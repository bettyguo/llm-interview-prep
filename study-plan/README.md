# Study plans

Three variants, plus the daily template. Pick the one closest to your situation; the plans share the same topic spine.

| Plan | For | Length |
|------|-----|--------|
| [Default 8-week](default-8-week.md) | Prepping cold; new grad or first AI/ML loop | 8 weeks |
| [Experienced 4-week](experienced-4-week.md) | Senior engineer with prior ML exposure | 4 weeks |
| [1-week cram](cram-1-week.md) | Loop is next week | 7 days |
| [Daily template](daily-template.md) | The rhythm you use inside any plan | per day |

## Honest expectations

- **8 weeks** ≈ 12–15 hr/week is enough for a strong candidate to feel exam-ready end-to-end. Less time still yields a good return; you just leave depth on the table.
- **4 weeks** assumes ~15–20 hr/week and prior fundamentals. The plan front-loads transformers/training/serving and depends on you skimming what you already know.
- **1 week cram** is for triage. You will not master new material. You will refresh, sharpen the openers for the 50 most-likely questions, and rehearse one or two system-design drills.

## How to know you're on track

- **End of week 1**: you can answer ten Topic-1 questions cold without checking.
- **End of week 3**: you can implement attention from scratch in NumPy without notes.
- **End of week 5**: you can walk through a small RAG design (drill D6 shape) in 30 minutes.
- **End of week 7**: you can do an unfamiliar system-design drill in 45 minutes timed.
- **End of week 8**: you have 5–7 polished behavioral stories.

If you fall behind, do not panic — re-prioritize toward the topics most likely to be in your loop (ask the recruiter what shape the loop is) and accept gaps elsewhere.
